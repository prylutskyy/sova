## What the measurements say

The main current failure is missed threats and category confusion, not binary over-alerting on the benign subset. Only 88 of 189 labeled threats formed a local intent; of those, just 58 received the expected threat category. Crypto and identity detectors are especially weak on this corpus. The payment detector often absorbs verification, identity, crypto, or other cases because the shared `verification_trap` cluster is sufficient by itself for `PAYMENT_CREDENTIAL_THEFT`.

There is also an impact risk: `MILITARY_SABOTAGE_RECRUITMENT` is checked first and can form from one `military_sabotage` cluster. The policy maps that class directly to `LOCK_INPUT`. A wrong class prediction can therefore impose a stronger action than a normal `WARN`, even when the message is a different kind of threat. `ChatSessionState` aggregates clusters and spans over as many as 30 messages for 15 minutes, including outbound user messages; unrelated or quoted context can combine with later messages unless direction and causal relevance are considered.

## Recommended improvements, in priority order

1. **Increase the benign and ambiguous holdout set.** Keep paraphrase families in one split. Add realistic delivery, banking, identity, crypto, military-news, quoted-threat, negation, and user-report examples. Aim for at least 40% benign/ambiguous scenarios in the final evaluation and keep the holdout untouched during tuning. Current holdout has only 11 benign cases.
2. **Fix the broad payment bucket.** Require evidence of an actual request to disclose or enter a sensitive payment credential, plus its context (who is asking, why, and where). A mere mention of verification, a card, an SMS code, or a wallet should not by itself make `PAYMENT_CREDENTIAL_THEFT`. Give identity, crypto, and verification discriminative evidence and score all candidate intents before selecting one instead of returning the first matching definition.
3. **Protect the high-impact lock action.** Require multiple independent, specific indicators of solicitation/recruitment or a concrete harmful task before classifying military sabotage. Use `WARN`/review for a single ambiguous military keyword or news/quotation context. Add an explicit negative/quoted-context gate before `LOCK_INPUT`.
4. **Use inbound, recent evidence for threat formation.** Do not let the user's own outbound text count as evidence that the interlocutor is attacking them. Preserve outbound text as context for language-model review, but separate its scoring. Apply per-message cluster weights, a short relevance window/decay, and reset/terminal rules so old unrelated clusters cannot combine into a new intent.
5. **Tune with class-level confusion matrices and cost-sensitive thresholds.** Track binary alert precision/recall separately from exact intent type and action severity. Set a higher precision requirement for `LOCK_INPUT` than `WARN`; tune thresholds on development/regressions and report holdout once at the end. Avoid trying to improve an already-zero measured binary FP count by lowering recall further.
6. **Make tests offline by default.** Mock `window.open` and external navigation in UI tests; assert the intended URL instead of allowing Happy DOM to fetch it. Keep all corpus cases synthetic.

Do not claim that the current measured zero false-positive count proves zero real-world false alarms. The sample is small, synthetic, and positive-heavy. First expand independently reviewed benign/ambiguous cases, then compare precision and recall before and after each rule change.
